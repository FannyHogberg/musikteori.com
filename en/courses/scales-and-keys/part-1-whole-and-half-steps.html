Part 1: Whole Steps and Half Steps

In the previous course you learned that a sharp raises a note by a half step and that a flat lowers a note by a half step.

Now we will take a closer look at the distance between notes. You need to know this to understand how scales and keys are built.

The distance between notes

The distance between two notes can be described in half steps and whole steps. On the piano it is easy to see the distances between notes.

Half step

A half step is the smallest distance between two notes on the piano.

On the piano you move a half step when you go from one key to the key right next to it - whether that key is white or black.

The distance between C and C sharp (C#) is a half step.

E and F are right next to each other on the piano. That is why the distance between them is a half step.

The distance between E and F is a half step.

The same is true between B and C.

The distance between B and C is a half step.

Question: Is the distance between F and F sharp (F#) a half step or a whole step?

Show answer

Answer: A half step. The keys are right next to each other, with no key between them.

Question: Which note do you land on if you go a half step up from B?

Show answer

Answer: C. B and C are right next to each other on the piano, so the distance is a half step.

Whole step

A whole step consists of two half steps.

On the piano this means there is one key between the starting note and the ending note.

The distance between C and D is a whole step. Between them lies C sharp (C#), so you go two half steps: C → C sharp → D.

The distance between D sharp (D#) and F is a whole step. Between them lies E, so you go two half steps: D sharp → E → F.

The distance between E and F sharp (F#) is a whole step. Between them lies F, so you go two half steps: E → F → F sharp.

Question: Which note do you land on if you go a whole step up from G?

Show answer

Answer: A. You go two half steps: G → G sharp (G#) → A.

Question: Is the distance between E and F a whole step because both keys are white?

Show answer

Answer: No, it is a half step. A whole step up from E is F sharp (F#): E → F → F sharp. It is the distance, not the color of the keys, that counts.

Why does this matter?

Whole steps and half steps are the building blocks of scales. It is the distances between the notes that determine how a scale sounds and which notes it contains.

Once you understand these distances, you can see how scales are built and why certain notes need to be raised or lowered with a sharp or a flat.

Remember

  • A half step is the distance from one key to the key right next to it.
  • A whole step consists of two half steps.
  • The color of the keys does not matter - it is the distance between the notes that counts.
  • Between E and F, and between B and C, it is a half step because the keys are right next to each other.

Frequently asked questions about whole and half steps

What is a half step in music?

A half step (also called a semitone) is the smallest distance between two notes on the piano - from one key to the key right next to it, whether it is white or black.

What is a whole step in music?

A whole step (also called a whole tone) consists of two half steps. On the piano there is one key between the starting note and the ending note, for example C to D.

Why is there no black key between E and F?

E and F are already only a half step apart, so there is no key between them. The same is true for B and C.

How many half steps are there in an octave?

An octave contains twelve half steps. You can count them on the piano from one C to the next C, including both white and black keys.
