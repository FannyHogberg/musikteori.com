Part 4: Octaves

You have already encountered octaves in the course - you have practiced notes in the one-line octave, and in the previous part we also saw the small octave in bass clef.
Now we will go through the different octaves and why they have names.

Review: What is an octave?

Do you remember from Part 1? The note names start over after B - the next note becomes C again. The distance from one note to the next note with the same name is called an octave.

C – D – E – F – G – A – B – C

one octave

A piano has several C's, several D's, several E's and so on. Two C's have the same note name, but are at different pitches and therefore sound different.

If someone says "play a C" - which C do they mean? There are several!

That is why notes are divided into different octaves, and each octave has its own name. This way you can distinguish between, for example, a bright C and a dark C.

The octaves on the piano

Each octave stretches from one C to the next C. Here are the most common octaves:

Tip: Turn your device to landscape mode to see the image better
  • Great octave
  • Small octave
  • One-line octave - the one we started with in Part 2
  • Two-line octave

There are more octaves beyond these, but these four are the most common ones to start with.

How do you write the notes?

To show which octave a note belongs to, the note name is written in different ways:

Tip: Turn your device to landscape mode to see the image better
  • Great octave - uppercase letters (C, D, E...)
  • Small octave - lowercase letters (c, d, e...)
  • One-line octave - lowercase letters with a 1 (c1, d1, e1...)
  • Two-line octave - lowercase letters with a 2 (c2, d2, e2...)
  • Three-line octave - lowercase letters with a 3 (c3, d3, e3...)

Question: If you play a D in the small octave, and then play the same note but one octave higher - in which octave do you end up?

Show answer

Answer: The one-line octave. A D one octave higher still has the note name D, but is in the next octave - the one-line octave.

Well done!

Now you know what an octave is, why octaves have names and how to write notes in different octaves. Remember to practice, practice, practice!
