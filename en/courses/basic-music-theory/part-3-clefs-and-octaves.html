Part 3: The Staff and the Bass Clef

In the previous part we learned the staff and used the treble clef to find where the notes go.
Now we will take a closer look at what a clef actually does and learn one more clef: the bass clef.

What is a clef?

Look at this staff. There is a note - but which note is it?

It is actually impossible to tell - we need a clef!

A clef is a symbol at the beginning of the staff that tells us which note is on which line. Without a clef it is just five lines - we have no idea what the note is called.

The Treble Clef

In the previous part we used the treble clef to find the notes' positions on the staff. Let us review!

Treble clef →

The treble clef curls around the second line from the bottom. This shows that a note on that line is G.

From there we can figure out where all the other notes are, just as we did in Part 2.

The Bass Clef

The second most common clef is called the bass clef.

Bass clef →

The bass clef's two dots sit on either side of the fourth line from the bottom (the second line from the top). This shows that a note on that line is F.

It works the same way as the treble clef. The clef gives us a reference point and from there we can figure out where all the other notes are.

Question: F is on the fourth line in bass clef. Which note is one step down, in the space below F?

Show answer

Answer: E. E comes right before F in the order of notes (C D E F G A B).

The clef determines the notes

Look at what happens if we place a note on the same position, but with different clefs:

With treble clef: B

With bass clef: D

The note stays in exactly the same position, but depending on which clef we use, that position gets a different note name.
With the treble clef the note is B. With the bass clef it is D.

That is why the clef is so important. It helps us know which notes belong to the lines and spaces.

Treble clef and bass clef together

Sometimes you see the treble clef and bass clef at the same time. This is common in piano music, where the music is written on two staves.

The right hand usually plays what is written in the treble clef and the left hand plays what is written in the bass clef.

Here you can see how the notes in the two clefs correspond to keys on the piano:

Look at the lowest notes in the image. In the bass clef they sit comfortably on the lines, but in the treble clef you would need very many extra lines below the staff. Those extra lines are called ledger lines. Ledger lines are short extra lines that are added when a note does not fit on the five regular lines. It works, but becomes hard to read if there are too many. That is why it is practical to have different clefs - so that the notes usually end up on the regular lines.

Well done!

Now you know two clefs! You understand how both the treble clef and the bass clef work, and you have seen that the clef determines which notes belong to the lines and spaces on the staff.
